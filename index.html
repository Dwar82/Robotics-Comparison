Recommendation — grades 6–8 group in Fresno, transitioning out of FLL

Top pick: VEX IQ

Best overall fit for your situation. All grades 6–8 compete together on the same team. VEXcode IQ Blocks is the most similar coding experience to LEGO SPIKE Word Blocks — students adapt in roughly one season. The plastic snap-together system is conceptually closer to LEGO than metal FTC parts. An active California VEX IQ scene means events near Fresno. The clear graduation path (VEX IQ → VEX V5 in high school) gives students a coherent multi-year trajectory. The catch: you'll need new hardware (~$850–$1,000 for one team, est.) with no SPIKE carryover, and if you run multiple small teams for a full class, kit costs multiply.

Runner-up: WRO RoboMission

If budget is the binding constraint, WRO is the only program where your existing SPIKE Prime hardware is fully valid. Season pass is ~$149 (est.) and you're up and running. All grades 6–8 fit the Junior division. The tradeoff is a smaller US community, no dedicated Fresno qualifier (nearest events are in Ontario, CA, ~3 hours away), and no formal US high school pathway. It's a viable interim step, but you'd likely face another transition when students hit high school.

Strong contender if you can manage the 6th-grade split: FTC

FTC is the most prestigious transition, has the best long-term high school pathway (FTC → FRC), and Fresno has a dedicated in-city FTC qualifier. But FTC firmly excludes 6th graders. If you're willing to run a separate lower-grade program for 6th graders (or accept a delayed entry), FTC is worth seriously considering for your 7th and 8th graders. The startup cost and technical jump are real, but FTC teams in NorCal are a strong community with experienced mentors.

One more year of FLL: viable but strategically risky

Zero transition effort, SPIKE carries over, and your team just won the Central CA Championship — you'd be competitive again. But FLL ends permanently after 2026–27. Your incoming 6th graders would then have zero years of experience in whatever program comes next. Delaying a year costs incoming students time to build that experience. If you do stay in FLL, use the year to run parallel VEX IQ or FTC training so the switch in 2027–28 is less abrupt.

Biggest single tradeoff: VEX IQ gives all grades 6–8 a home together and has the smoothest coding transition, but requires ~$850–$1,000 in new hardware per team with no SPIKE carryover. FTC has the best long-term ecosystem (FTC → FRC is the gold standard HS pathway), a local Fresno qualifier, and is the most recognized program in STEM colleges and scholarships — but your 6th graders are locked out and the startup cost and technical jump are the highest of any option.
Watch out for:
  • FTC's hard age floor: Most 6th graders turn 12 mid-year at best. Verify individual students' birth dates carefully — many will not qualify even in spring. You may end up with a fractured team.
  • SPIKE Prime sunset: LEGO Education is selling SPIKE Prime sets only until June 30, 2026. If you need replacement sets for a WRO or FLL season, order immediately. After that date, you'll need the secondary market.
  • VEX IQ → V5 transition: Your current 6th graders will face another hardware change (plastic IQ → metal V5) when they hit high school. It's a known and manageable step, but budget for it.
  • WRO's thin Fresno network: Without a local WRO coach community or Fresno-area events, you'll carry more of the coaching and logistics burden alone than in FLL or VEX where local partners and veteran coaches are active.
  • FLL Future Edition: FIRST launched a new "Future Edition" of FLL for grades 6–8 using new LEGO CS&AI hardware (not SPIKE Prime). It runs for 2026–27 but is also the final FLL season and uses different hardware — not a substitute for the long-term decision you're making.
